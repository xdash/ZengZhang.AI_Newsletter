# This elite matchmaker charges clients up to $100,000. Now, they’re building an AI

Matchmaking is an ancient dating process that stretches back thousands of years. But as online dating fatigue has begun to dominate the modern-day discourse around finding love, one company is betting that matchmaking will see a revival, and they’ve spent years developing a tool to make it happen: an [AI](https://www.fastcompany.com/section/artificial-intelligence "AI") matchmaker named Tai.

One might argue that all modern dating apps aim to serve as a kind of matchmaker; an intermediary whose purpose is to connect two singles with each other. But Adam Cohen-Aslatei, CEO of the matchmaking company Three Day Rule, says dating apps still put the onus on users to choose the right partners based on what the algorithm serves. In contrast, he explains, a traditional matchmaker uses their in-depth knowledge of the client to facilitate that process, selecting matches that aren’t just compatible on a screen, but in real life.

Now, Three Day Rule is rolling out its first-ever app designed to bring that human-centric matchmaking experience to a broader client base. The app leverages multiple AI models built upon 15 years of matchmaking data collected by the company. While Three Day Rule’s elite “white-glove” matchmaking services typically run clients around $2,000 per month, its AI matchmaker is available starting for free.

AI features have become increasingly popular on existing dating apps in recent months. But Cohen-Aslatei says none of them are fixing the larger problem: Self-serve dating apps just don’t work very well. Traditional dating apps are effective 9-11% of the time, he claims, compared with an 70-80% success rate for traditional matchmaking.

“Matchmakers aren’t self-serve; they are serving you,” Cohen-Aslatei says. “They are doing all the work for you. And they’re saying, ‘I know you better than you know yourself. I know what you need for long term relationships. And I’m not going to let you waste your time on people that are candy, I’m going to give you the full meal.’”

## Is matchmaking the new swiping?

For many singles searching for love, AI-powered tools are becoming an integral part of the swiping process. Hinge [now uses AI to help users craft better profile prompt responses](https://www.fastcompany.com/91259831/hinge-will-now-use-ai-to-grade-your-dating-profile-prompts); Tinder [is set to roll out AI-powered personalized matches](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/06/tinder-will-try-ai-powered-matching-as-the-dating-app-continues-to-lose-users/); the[ AI assistant app Rizz uses AI exclusively to help users come up with responses on dating apps](https://www.businessinsider.com/rizz-dating-app-ai-analyzes-text-screenshot-conversations-advice-2024-6); the AI relationship advice app Meeno helps men find love; and Bumble founder Whitney Wolfe Herd even [announced that the company is currently working on its own AI matchmaking service](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/whitney-wolfe-herd-bumble-ai-398779bb?gaa_at=eafs&gaa_n=ASWzDAiKm8THuwNfX3f28vQmowlSIcgDTCpeW9k_uv1cAjjK5GhUapOGtsdCV_lCk7c%3D&gaa_ts=68dfd8cf&gaa_sig=E5J5q41MwK_NY8nxdSn-xcWUZC6yaBCg2rJqZn__6UtkJgEnJPnLVDlEhH-1wCJA8KWSVih17xZ8NvKZpdDeHQ%3D%3D). 

Cohen-Aslatei has seen it all. He previously worked at dating apps including The Meet Group, Zoosk, and Bumble’s gay dating app Chappy, and even founded and sold his own app called S’More. But, over the past few years, he’s become convinced that matchmaking is the most effective dating option out there.
